**Who looked, and how.** Claude, in the same session that built these parts, so these are not
fresh eyes and not a human playtest. The user hadn't asked for agents, so no independent critic agent
was run. What was looked at:

- the real game in the browser pane at 1280×720: intro cards, a train passing on 2-2, the crane hook
  on 3-6, the dance floor and strobe on 4-2, the Frame's eyes on 5-8, the Conductor's landing on 2-8,

Nobody has judged the sound by ear or how the new chapters feel to a person yet. That is the next
critic pass's job (an independent agent, a human, or both).

## Open, for the next pass

- **Par on 1-8 (53 s)** comes from the bot, which got slower when heavy reds stopped being
  stun-lockable. A person will likely beat it easily; tighten it after a human run.
- **The strobe** keeps the room dark for 2/3 of every 0.6 game s cycle; standing still to think in a
  strobe room means thinking in the dark for many real seconds. Intended ("reds still glow"), but it
  could feel unfair. Fewer flashes turns it off.
- **The Gauntlet** gets hard fast: the bot falls in the third arena (waves 11–15). A person may go
  further, or may find the jump from wave 10 (first Brute, Mirrors, echo) steep.
- **Chapter pay** is flat (425–463 per chapter, 672 for chapter 5) where the design asked for +25% a
  chapter. The total lands on the 70% target, so it was left alone.
- **The Foreman over the edge** (3-8's challenge) never happens in the bot's runs: it shoots it down.
  Whether people find the edge plan on their own is a question for a playtest.
- **Sound** for the new events (trains, the beat, the hook, katana, reflections) is synthesized and
  untested by ear.
